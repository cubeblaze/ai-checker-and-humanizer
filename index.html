Accuracy — and an important caveat about it
The primary detector is trained on 11 different LLM architectures (GPT‑2/3/4, ChatGPT, Mistral, MPT, LLaMA‑2, Cohere), not one — that’s what lets it generalize to models it never saw, like Claude or Gemini, rather than only recognizing ChatGPT’s style. Swapping in that model took a fresh-text test from 1/6 to 5/6. Across 19 samples it scores 16/19, with 2 false positives on dense academic and historical human prose.

The caveat: every one of those samples was 70–130 words — below the length this tool now refuses to judge. That threshold exists because of a measured effect: one identical human passage scored 90% AI at 70 words and 11% at 134 words. Short text collapses toward “AI” regardless of who wrote it. So the accuracy figure above was measured in exactly the range where the detector is least trustworthy, and I have not verified it on 200+ word text. Longer input should behave better, not worse — but that is an expectation, not a measurement. Use this as a signal, never as proof about a person.

Transformations

What this actually does — and its real limit
Every transformation here is lexical, structural or punctuation-level — nothing is invented. Measured against the current detector, that moves the score by roughly 1 point, not 50. That number went down, not up, when the detector improved: the model now doing the scoring was trained with adversarial hard-negative mining specifically to resist this kind of surface rewriting, so word swaps and sentence-splitting barely register. I also tested a small (0.5B) language model running fully in-browser to do real generative rewriting instead of rule-based swaps — it made the writing worse (more corporate, not less) and moved the detector score by zero, while costing a slow multi-minute download and 10+ seconds per paragraph. That's not shipped. There is currently no way to reliably beat this detector by rewriting text with it in the loop, and any tool that claims otherwise should be treated with suspicion. Read the output before using it, and remember that passing a detector is not the same as the text being yours.
